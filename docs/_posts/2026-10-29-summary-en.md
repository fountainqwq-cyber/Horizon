### [China smartphone shipments fall 15.2% in Q3 2026 as memory costs suppress demand](https://www.ithome.com/1/019/400.htm) ⭐️ 8.6/10

IDC&\#x27;s Q3 2026 China smartphone tracker estimates shipments at about 58.06 million units, down 15.2% year on year, a sharper decline than the roughly 4% fall in the first half. IDC attributes the drop to higher memory and component costs that lifted device prices and forced configuration changes, suppressing demand and lengthening replacement cycles. The mix shifted toward higher price bands, while foldable shipments fell 32.7% to 1.77 million units, the fourth consecutive quarterly decline \(IDC estimate; IT Home report: https://www.ithome.com/1/019/400.htm\).

rss · IT Home industry reports \(strict trend filter\) · Oct 29, 03:36

**「Impact」** IDC&\#x27;s figures show a cost-driven mix shift: share above US$600 rose 7.1 percentage points year on year and US$400–600 rose 4.6 points, while sub-US$200 share fell 13.9 points; Huawei reached 21.1% share on double-digit shipment growth. That points to volume risk concentrated in entry-tier Android and long-tail brands, and to channel and component-order exposure as OEMs trim low-end configurations. IDC forecasts no meaningful recovery in Q4 2026 and continued decline through most of 2027 as high memory costs persist. Inference: memory and component inflation, not weak flagship appetite, is now the main drag on China smartphone volumes; the report does not give absolute ASPs or channel inventory levels.

**「Details」** Methodological note: IDC&\#x27;s figures are market shipment estimates, not sell-through; the source reports price-band share changes and foldable shipments but not absolute ASPs or channel inventory. In foldables, Huawei held more than 70% share, Samsung 8.0% and Xiaomi 6.4%, per IDC.

### [Samsung says 2027 HBM capacity is sold out, expects tight DRAM supply into 2028](https://www.ithome.com/1/019/430.htm) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On its 2026Q3 earnings call \(October 29, 2026\), Samsung Electronics said its 2027 HBM capacity has been sold out at target prices and that it expects overall DRAM supply to remain tight in 2028 — both company forecasts, not independently verified. For Q3, Samsung reported single-digit percentage growth in both DRAM and NAND bit shipments, with ASPs up 20% and nearly 10% respectively. It guided Q4 DRAM shipments up 15–20% and NAND up about 15%.

rss · IT Home industry reports \(strict trend filter\) · Oct 29, 05:50

**「Contract lock-ups reshape allocation and device BOM costs」** Per Samsung, its memory business has signed long-term agreements \(LTAs\) with 10 customers and is negotiating multi-year contracts with 10 more, aiming to lock 60–70% of output; interest has spread to mobile, automotive and networking buyers, and some customers want additional supply within contract terms. The reported ASP gains imply higher memory content cost for smartphones, PCs and servers, though Samsung also said the overall memory market remains oversupplied — so pricing pressure is product-dependent rather than uniform. Inference: as multi-year contracts absorb a larger share of output, spot availability for smaller device makers could tighten first, while contracted buyers gain allocation visibility.

**「HBM4 mix and foundry capacity」** Samsung said HBM4 sales grew more than threefold quarter-on-quarter in Q3 and now account for 60% of its total HBM revenue, and that its HBM market share has reached parity with its overall DRAM share ahead of plan. Its foundry business plans increased investment in Korean and US fabs with co-located advanced packaging, is evaluating further fab expansion, and expects sub-8nm node utilization to exceed 100%. Caveat: these are company disclosures and targets; the 2027 sell-out and 2028 tightness are forward-looking claims, and HBM demand is concentrated in AI servers, so the read-through to consumer devices is indirect.
