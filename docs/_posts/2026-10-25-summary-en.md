### [Block unveils Dessa voice model, access limited to safety researchers](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/blocks-new-voice-model-sounds-eerily-human/) ⭐️ 6.2/10

Block’s research lab released Dessa, a full-duplex voice model that can speak and listen simultaneously and runs locally on consumer hardware such as a MacBook M4 Max or better, the company says. Block claims Dessa is trained entirely on human speech and a single NVIDIA GPU rack, with emotion built into the model rather than a text pipeline; independent evaluation is not yet available. Dessa is not publicly accessible, and Block has called for safety researchers to test it.

rss · TechCrunch AI · Oct 24, 16:00

**「Impact」** The on-device design could let Block and Square add lower-cost voice interactions without sending audio to cloud models, relevant to ordering and other small-business services. Its immediate effect is limited because outside developers and consumers cannot use it yet.

**「Details」** Dessa was announced on Oct. 24, 2026. Block did not detail public pricing or a general release date; access is currently limited to safety researchers. The report compares it with OpenAI’s GPT-Live full-duplex model.

### [Global PC shipments fall 21% in Q3 2026 as component costs lift prices, Counterpoint says](https://www.ithome.com/1/017/508.htm) ⭐️ 8.7/10

Counterpoint Research preliminary data show global PC shipments of 57 million units in Q3 2026, down 21% year over year and 13.8% quarter over quarter, IT Home reported on 24 October 2026. The firm attributes the decline to persistently high memory and other component costs, which pushed PC prices up and weighed on demand. Consumer demand stayed weak as higher prices gave buyers less reason to replace devices, while commercial demand held up better on enterprise refresh and Windows migration but did not offset the overall contraction. Counterpoint also flags a high year-earlier base: Q3 2025 shipments were lifted by buying ahead of the Windows 10 end-of-support deadline and tariff-related inventory adjustments.

rss · IT Home industry reports \(strict trend filter\) · Oct 24, 13:44

**「Vendor divergence and cost pressure」** Per the report, the top five vendors took 76.7% of shipments and their performances diverged: Lenovo shipped 15 million units \(26.3% share, -23% YoY\), HP 10.3 million \(18.1%, -31%, the steepest fall among the top six\), Dell 7.6 million \(13.3%, -25%\), Apple 5.6 million \(-11%, share up to 9.8% from 8.7%\) and Asus 5.2 million \(-7% YoY but +19.4% QoQ, share up to 9.2% from 7.8%\); all other vendors combined shipped 13.3 million units, down 16%. The evidence points to share shifting toward vendors with milder declines while the whole market shrinks. Inference: sustained memory cost inflation raises average selling prices and squeezes consumer replacement budgets, so OEMs face weaker sell-through and cautious channel stocking, while memory suppliers capture more value per unit and contract manufacturers see lower build volumes; commercial-heavy vendors are cushioned but not insulated.

**「Data caveats」** The figures are preliminary Counterpoint estimates rather than final shipment data, and the reported year-over-year drop is amplified by an unusually strong Q3 2025 comparison tied to the Windows 10 support deadline and tariff-driven inventory moves. The article gives no absolute ASP figures or channel inventory levels, so the price effect on demand is described qualitatively.
