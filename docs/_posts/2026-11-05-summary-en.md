### [Meta acquires AI coding startup Conductor](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/meta-acquires-ai-coding-startup-conductor/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Meta has acquired Conductor, an AI coding startup whose product deploys multiple agents into parallel workspaces for software projects, Meta confirmed to TechCrunch without disclosing deal terms. Founder and CEO Charlie Holtz said Conductor&\#x27;s core features will keep working as before until early next year, but the cloud service shuts down on November 20, after which user cloud data will be deleted. Holtz said the team has &quot;something new&quot; planned for customers by early next year.

rss · TechCrunch AI · Nov 4, 21:10

**「Why it matters」** Conductor&\#x27;s cloud users face a hard migration deadline to keep their project data, while Meta gains a multi-agent coding tool that Holtz said he will work on with chief AI officer Alexandr Wang and Superintelligence Labs product head Nat Friedman. The deal follows Meta&\#x27;s August launch of its own coding agent, Muse Code.

**「Context」** Conductor was founded in 2024 and launched its core product last summer; its agents come from labs including Anthropic and OpenAI and can be deployed in the cloud, locally, or via a phone. Holtz described the acquisition on the company blog and on X; Axios first reported the news. Meta has also said its Muse agents passed 10 million users.

### [Google to Cut Search for Schools That Don&\#x27;t Opt In to AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/google-faces-backlash-for-plan-that-would-turn-off-search-for-schools-that-restrict-ai/) ⭐️ 7.5/10

Google said K-12 schools using its education software will lose access to Search unless they opt in to AI tools. Chris Phillips, Google&\#x27;s vice president for education, told The New York Times that &quot;Search and AI are deeply integrated,&quot; so students at schools that decline AI tools like Gemini &quot;will not have access to search&quot; and will instead see a message from their school. The plan drew backlash on X from Senator Bernie Sanders, who called it &quot;extortion,&quot; Senator Richard Blumenthal, former FTC chair Lina Khan and New York City mayor Zohran Mamdani. Google responded that it had turned AI products off by default after educator feedback and that schools can enable Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Search individually.

rss · TechCrunch AI · Nov 4, 16:17

**「Why it matters」** The policy affects millions of K-12 students whose schools rely on Google&\#x27;s Chromebooks and education tools, where switching to another search engine is not a simple fix. It also puts pressure on Google over how much control vendors hold over classroom tools and whether students can avoid AI-generated search results.

**「Additional context」** Google said it is developing a &quot;Guided Learning&quot; feature for student Search accounts intended to walk students through solving problems rather than giving answers outright. Official terms, timing and any exception process for schools were not specified in the report.

### [Counterpoint: Q3 2026 DRAM revenue nears $200B on price-led supercycle](https://www.ithome.com/1/022/677.htm) ⭐️ 9.2/10

Counterpoint Research’s Global Memory Market Tracker estimates Q3 2026 global DRAM revenue at close to $200 billion, up 369% year on year—nearly 5x the year-earlier level. The firm says growth was price-driven: unit shipments rose only about 4% quarter on quarter, while average selling price reached $1.9 per Gb. It labels the upturn a price-led supercycle, with AI demand making memory a scarce resource and buyers prioritizing supply access over price negotiation.

rss · IT Home industry reports \(strict trend filter\) · Nov 5, 05:48

**「Impact」** Evidence: Counterpoint reports Samsung held 38% DRAM share; SK Hynix grew revenue more than 40% QoQ and narrowed the gap; Micron grew but lagged; CXMT fell to 9% from 10% and is expected to face pressure as rivals ship HBM4. DDR and LPDDR revenue rose about 500% and nearly 400% YoY. Inference: sustained high memory prices raise component costs for smartphone, PC and edge-device makers, while favoring memory suppliers with HBM exposure and long-term agreement coverage; device vendors may face margin pressure and tighter supply allocation.

**「Details」** Counterpoint’s figures are estimates, and its HBM share outlook is an analyst expectation rather than confirmed shipment data. It forecasts memory prices will stay strong next year, supported by long-term agreements, with major new fabs not starting until 2028. Source: https://www.ithome.com/1/022/677.htm

### [Global tablet market contracts 23% in 2026Q3, Omdia says](https://www.ithome.com/1/022/587.htm) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Omdia reported that global tablet shipments fell 23% year over year to 32 million units in 2026Q3, the lowest quarterly total in nearly three years, after a 10% decline in the prior quarter. The research firm said the Middle East, China and Western Europe were hit hardest, and that supply constraints and tablets&\#x27; declining priority in vendors&\#x27; 2026-2027 plans point to tighter supply next year. Omdia forecasts no substantial tablet-market recovery before 2028.

rss · IT Home industry reports \(strict trend filter\) · Nov 5, 03:34

**「Supply-chain pressure」** Omdia&\#x27;s figures point to a shrinking demand base and weaker order visibility for tablet contract manufacturers and component suppliers. Omdia also notes Lenovo again gained share amid the downturn, indicating that competition is shifting toward share defense as overall volumes fall. The reported decline in vendor prioritization suggests brands are reallocating resources away from tablets; for the supply chain, that implies inventory and capacity planning should assume lower tablet volumes rather than a near-term rebound. This is an inference from Omdia&\#x27;s shipment and supply-constraint commentary, not a separately quantified forecast.

**「Scope and source」** Methodological note: the source provides global shipment totals and qualitative regional impact but no detailed vendor share table. The 2028 recovery view is an Omdia forecast. Source: https://www.ithome.com/1/022/587.htm
